# ADR-Basics
ADR-Basics.

Introduction

Pharmacovigilance is the science and activities  related to detecting, assessing, understanding, and preventing adverse effects or other medicine-related problems.

An adverse drug reaction (ADR) is a harmful and unintended response to a medicinal product.

Project Objective

The objective of this project is to demonstrate the basic process of identifying suspected adverse drug reactions using fictional patient scenarios.

Types of ADRs

Type A (Augmented): Predictable reactions related to the pharmacological action of a medicine.

Type B (Bizarre): Uncommon and generally unpredictable reactions.

Type C (Chronic): Reactions associated with prolonged use.

Type D (Delayed): Reactions that appear after some time.

Type E (End of use): Reactions associated with withdrawal.

Type F (Failure): Unexpected failure of treatment.

These are commonly taught categories of ADRs.

Fictional Patient Case

Patient: Female, 45 years old Medical condition: Hypertension Suspected medicine: Drug A, 5 mg once daily Reported events: Nausea and dizziness Timeline: Both symptoms reportedly began three days after treatment started.
This is a fictional training case. It does not represent a real patient.

ADR Identification Process

Step 1: Identify the reported events
The patient reports nausea and dizziness. Record both events separately.

Step 2: Review the medication timeline
The reported symptoms started after the medicine was initiated. This temporal relationship may be relevant, but it does not prove causation.

Step 3: Identify the suspected medicine
Drug A is the suspected medicine based on the reported timeline.

Step 4: Consider other possible causes
Review the patient’s medical history, concomitant medicines, underlying conditions, and any available clinical findings.

Step 5: Identify missing information

Exact treatment start date
Exact onset date for each symptom
Other medicines being taken
Relevant medical history
Whether the medicine was stopped or continued
Whether symptoms resolved
Clinical evaluation and outcome
Seriousness information
Preliminary Assessment
Nausea and dizziness are suspected adverse events reported after Drug A was started.

The available information is insufficient to establish a causal relationship. Further information and appropriate clinical assessment are needed.

Follow-up Questions

When did the patient start Drug A?
When did each symptom begin?
Was Drug A stopped or continued?
Did the symptoms improve after any change in treatment?
Was the patient taking any other medicines?
Was there any hospitalization or other serious medical outcome?

Causality Assessment

Causality assessment considers factors such as the timing of the event, alternative explanations, the response to withdrawal or reintroduction of the medicine, and available clinical evidence.
WHO-UMC and the Naranjo algorithm are examples of causality assessment methods.
A formal causality category cannot be assigned reliably from this limited fictional case alone.

ADR Reporting in India

The Pharmacovigilance Programme of India (PvPI) supports the monitoring and reporting of suspected adverse drug reactions to improve medicine safety.
Suspected ADRs should be documented and reported through appropriate established reporting channels. Proof of causality is not always required before reporting a suspected reaction.
